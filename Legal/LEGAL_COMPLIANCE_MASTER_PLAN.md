## 🏛️ Legal Framework: MN + Federal Requirements

### Minnesota-Specific Requirements

#### 1. Business Registration & Licensing

- **Entity Type**: LLC (GFV LLC)
- **DBA Filing**: "Good Flippin Design" registered with MN Secretary of State
- **Business License**: Minneapolis/county-level (verify current status)
- **Tax Registration**: MN Dept of Revenue - Sales Tax (if applicable for software)
- **Annual Renewal**: MN LLC annual renewal requirement

#### 2. Minnesota Consumer Protection Laws

- **MN Statute 325F**: Unfair trade practices
- **MN Statute 325D**: False advertising
- **Plain Language Requirement**: Contracts must be reasonably understandable
- **Right to Cancel**: 3-day cooling-off period for certain contracts

#### 3. Minnesota Data Privacy

- **MN Statute 325M**: Data breach notification (if customer data stored)
- **Minnesota Government Data Practices Act**: If working with government entities

#### 4. Professional Requirements

- **No License Required**: Web development generally unlicensed in MN
- **Exception**: Engineering work may require PE license (avoid structural claims)
- **Professional Liability**: Strongly recommended E&O insurance

### Federal Requirements

#### 1. Tax & Employment

- **EIN**: Employer Identification Number (already obtained)
- **1099 Reporting**: If hiring contractors
- **Quarterly Estimated Taxes**: Self-employment tax obligations
- **Business Deductions**: Proper documentation for IRS compliance

#### 2. Intellectual Property

- **Copyright**: Automatic on original work; registration for litigation
- **Trademark**: Protect brand name (consider USPTO registration)
- **DMCA Compliance**: Safe harbor provisions if hosting user content
- **Work-for-Hire Clauses**: Essential in client contracts

#### 3. Federal Consumer Protection

- **FTC Act Section 5**: Prohibits unfair/deceptive practices
- **CAN-SPAM Act**: Email marketing compliance
- **TCPA**: Telephone/text marketing restrictions
- **ADA Title III**: Website accessibility (increasing litigation risk)

#### 4. Industry-Specific Compliance

- **HIPAA**: If handling healthcare data (Business Associate Agreement required)
- **GDPR**: If serving EU clients (rare for US-only freelancer)
- **CCPA/CPRA**: California privacy law (if CA clients)
- **SOC 2**: For enterprise clients requiring security certification

#### 5. Contract Law (UCC & Common Law)

- **Statute of Frauds**: Contracts >$500 should be in writing
- **Electronic Signatures**: ESIGN Act allows digital contracts
- **Force Majeure**: COVID-era relevance for project delays
- **Limitation of Liability**: Generally enforceable if reasonable

## 🎯 Key Legal Concepts for Tech Consultancy

### 1. Work-for-Hire vs. License

**Issue**: Who owns the code after delivery?

**Options**:

- **Work-for-Hire**: Client owns all IP immediately (higher risk, charge premium)
- **License Grant**: You retain ownership, grant perpetual license to client (preferred)
- **Joint Ownership**: Hybrid model (complex, avoid unless necessary)

**Recommendation**: Retain copyright, grant broad license. Protects your portfolio rights.

### 2. Limitation of Liability

**Purpose**: Cap damages if project fails

**Typical Clauses**:

- Cap at contract value (or 2x contract value)
- Exclude consequential damages (lost profits, etc.)
- Exclude gross negligence (always liable for intentional harm)

**Enforceability**: Generally upheld if reasonable and conspicuous

### 3. Warranty Disclaimers

**Standard Approach**:

- Provide 30-90 day bug fix warranty
- Disclaim all other warranties (merchantability, fitness for purpose)
- Use UPPERCASE or bold for conspicuousness

**Sample**: "EXCEPT AS EXPRESSLY PROVIDED, ALL WORK IS PROVIDED 'AS IS' WITHOUT WARRANTY OF ANY KIND."

### 4. Indemnification

**Mutual Protection**: Each party protects the other from certain claims

**Developer Indemnifies Client**:

- IP infringement (code you wrote violates someone's copyright)
- Gross negligence or willful misconduct

**Client Indemnifies Developer**:

- Content provided by client (text, images, data)
- Client's use of deliverables beyond scope
- Client's business operations

### 5. Payment Terms

**Best Practices**:

- 50% deposit upfront (or 1/3 for large projects)
- Milestone payments for projects >$10K
- Net 15 for final invoice
- Late fees (1.5%/month or legal max)
- Right to suspend work for non-payment
- Retain ownership until final payment

### 6. Termination Rights

**Developer Can Terminate**:

- Non-payment after 15 days notice
- Client interference prevents performance
- Material breach by client

**Client Can Terminate**:

- For convenience (with kill fee: 25-50% of remaining balance)
- For cause (developer material breach)

**Both Parties**:

- Mutual agreement
- Force majeure (extended impossibility)

## 🚨 Common Legal Pitfalls to Avoid

### 1. Verbal Agreements ❌

**Problem**: No proof of terms, scope, or payment
**Solution**: Every engagement requires signed written contract

### 2. Scope Creep Without Change Orders ❌

**Problem**: Free work, disputes over "what was included"
**Solution**: Define scope precisely, require Change Order for additions

### 3. No IP Assignment Clause ❌

**Problem**: Client thinks they own code, you think you do
**Solution**: Explicit IP ownership section in every contract

### 4. Guaranteeing Results ❌

**Problem**: "We'll get you to #1 on Google" = potential fraud claim
**Solution**: Promise efforts, not outcomes ("best practices SEO")

### 5. Working Without Insurance ❌

**Problem**: One lawsuit bankrupts the business
**Solution**: $1M E&O minimum, $2M general liability

### 6. Misclassifying Workers ❌

**Problem**: IRS reclassifies contractor as employee, back taxes + penalties
**Solution**: Use proper IC agreements, avoid control indicators

### 7. Storing Client Data Insecurely ❌

**Problem**: Data breach = liability + reputation damage
**Solution**: Encryption, secure cloud storage, access controls

### 8. Ignoring Accessibility ❌

**Problem**: ADA lawsuits increasing (especially against web developers)
**Solution**: WCAG 2.1 AA compliance standard, disclaim "perfect" accessibility

### 9. No Non-Solicitation Clause ❌

**Problem**: Client hires you, then hires your subcontractors directly
**Solution**: 1-year non-solicitation of your team members

### 10. Unclear Dispute Resolution ❌

**Problem**: Expensive litigation over $5K project
**Solution**: Mediation required, arbitration optional, venue selection

## 💼 Insurance Requirements

### Essential Policies

#### 1. Professional Liability (E&O)

**Coverage**: Errors, omissions, negligence in professional services
**Minimum**: $1M per occurrence, $2M aggregate
**Cost**: ~$500-2000/year for solo freelancer
**Carrier Examples**: Hiscox, Insureon, Next Insurance

#### 2. General Liability

**Coverage**: Bodily injury, property damage (rare for remote work)
**Minimum**: $1M per occurrence
**Cost**: ~$300-800/year
**Bundling**: Often packaged with E&O (BOP = Business Owner's Policy)

#### 3. Cyber Liability (Recommended)

**Coverage**: Data breaches, ransomware, GDPR fines
**When Required**: If storing customer data, especially healthcare
**Cost**: ~$500-1500/year

#### 4. Business Property (Optional)

**Coverage**: Computer equipment, office contents
**When Needed**: Home office with >$10K in equipment
**Alternative**: Check homeowner's/renter's policy rider

### Policy Features to Verify

- **Tail Coverage**: Protection after policy expires for prior work
- **Defense Costs**: Outside policy limits vs. inside limits
- **Retroactive Date**: Covers work done before policy start
- **Subcontractor Coverage**: If you hire help
- **Contract Review**: Insurer reviews client contracts (some offer this)
